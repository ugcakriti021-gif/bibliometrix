How to read the map

Each cluster of co-occurring keywords is scored on two dimensions (Cobo et al., 2011): centrality — how strongly the theme interacts with the rest of the field (relevance), and density — how cohesive and developed the theme is internally (development). The dashed cross marks the mean of the two scores; the four quadrants give the classic reading.

MOTOR THEMES ↗
High centrality, high density. Well developed and central: the themes that drive and structure the research field.
NICHE THEMES ↖
High density, low centrality. Internally mature but peripheral: specialised sub-fields with their own methods and vocabulary.
BASIC THEMES ↘
High centrality, low density. Transversal, cross-cutting foundations: important everywhere but not yet a coherent sub-literature.
EMERGING / DECLINING ↙
Low on both. Margin of the field: either an embryonic research front or a fading topic — time-series analysis tells which.

Synthesis: the shape of this research field

The field shows a clear core–periphery structure. A single dominant motor theme — human · health care quality · humans — carries almost half of the mapped keyword mass and sits high on both centrality and density: this literature is mature and it is the hub everything connects to. Around it orbit (i) a methodologically mature but peripheral outcome niche (mortality · time factor · time factors), (ii) a large transversal descriptor layer (article · female · male) that glues studies together without forming a theory of its own, and (iii) a marginal implementation cluster (practice guideline · protocol compliance · clinical practice) at the emerging/declining margin. In plain language: the community knows that care quality matters and measures it in humans, has strong tools for time-to-event outcomes, reports sex-demographics routinely — but the question that arguably matters most for patients, whether practice actually follows guidelines, is still outside the core.

Recommendations

  • Bridge the niche to the core. The highest-value gap is between the mortality/time-factor niche and the health-care-quality motor: studies linking time-to-event outcomes to quality-improvement frameworks connect a mature method to the field's centre of gravity.
  • Test the guideline cluster over time before naming it. Its position (low centrality, low density, tiny bubble) fits both “emerging” and “declining”. Run thematicEvolution() in bibliometrix (or split the corpus in two periods): rising centrality would mark it as the field's next frontier — the known guideline-to-practice “care gap”.
  • Treat female/male/article as covariates, not as a theme. Their high centrality with low density is the signature of indexing descriptors shared by all studies. Watch whether their density grows: that would signal a maturing sex/gender-equity research front.
  • Monitor the motor for saturation. A motor theme whose density plateaus while new terms accumulate elsewhere is a field preparing to shift; periodic re-mapping (e.g. every 2–3 publication years) makes the shift visible early.
  • Replace the digitised values with exact ones. Re-run the companion script enhanced_thematic_map.R on your Scopus export to obtain exact centrality, density and cluster cardinalities.

Methods & caveats

  • Framework: strategic diagram of Cobo et al. (2011); implementation: thematicMap() of the bibliometrix R package (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017; Aria et al., 2026), as served by biblioshiny.
  • Bubble centres, radii and the median cross were extracted pixel-exactly from your screenshot by colour-blob detection; coordinates are expressed in centred relative units (0 = median cross, ±1 = panel edge). Quadrant membership is unambiguous because all four clusters sit deep inside their quadrants.
  • Weights are bubble-area shares (a proxy for theme size; bibliometrix scales radius by log-frequency). Exact frequencies, centrality and density values require re-running the analysis on the source file — use enhanced_thematic_map.R in this folder with your Scopus export.
  • The watermark logo overlapping the original screenshot's lower-right corner was detected and excluded; it is not a cluster.
